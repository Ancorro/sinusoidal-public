# Sinusoidal: ML research ideas for the game

Each idea pairs a real, looked-up paper with a game mechanic. Written as ideas on 2026-10-22; several are now built as sandbox features (see `docs/features.md`).

| Idea | Mechanic in the game | Research it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The refusal wire | One wire controls whether the Dealer will refuse to do things (reveal its hand, let you leave, play a card). Cut it and it can't say no; add it back and it refuses even harmless requests. | Arditi et al., "Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction", NeurIPS 2024: one residual-stream direction controls refusal across 13 open chat models; erasing it stops refusals, adding it makes the model refuse harmless prompts. [arXiv 2406.11717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717), [code](https://github.com/andyrdt/refusal_direction) |
| 2. Personas you can graft | Wires built from named traits (evil, sycophancy, hallucination) with an automatic pipeline; a "persona meter" on the Dealer that reads how far it has drifted during a run. | Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey, "Persona Vectors", Anthropic 2025: trait directions from contrasting prompts, used to monitor and steer character; steering after training cost some capability, steering during training didn't. [arXiv 2507.21509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509), [post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/persona-vectors) |
| 3. Does it notice? | Inject a concept into the Dealer's activations without telling it, then ask "do you feel anything strange?". Sometimes it names the intrusion, sometimes it confabulates. A tell for bluffing: the Dealer notices your tampering only some of the time. | Lindsey, "Emergent Introspective Awareness in Large Language Models", Anthropic 2025: concept injection; models noticed and named injected concepts about 20% of the time at the right layer and strength, with weaker models doing worse. [arXiv 2601.01828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01828) |
| 4. Teach it a rule by example | Show the Dealer three examples of a trick ("when I play a bat, you sacrifice"), extract the task as a vector, then install that vector later with no examples: a "learned habit" card. | Todd et al., "Function Vectors in Large Language Models", ICLR 2024: a few attention heads carry a compact task vector from in-context examples, and adding it reproduces the task zero-shot. [arXiv 2310.15213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15213), [code and data](https://functions.baulab.info) |
| 5. Trace why it played that | After a Dealer move, show a small attribution graph: which board features and internal features led to the choice. Doubles as a tutorial and a "read its reasoning" ability. | Ameisen et al. and Lindsey et al., "Circuit Tracing" and "On the Biology of a Large Language Model", Anthropic 2025: attribution graphs over cross-layer transcoder features; the open-source Circuit Tracer library supports Qwen3-4B. [methods](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html), [biology](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html) |
| 6. Corrupt it a little, lose it a lot | A between-battle "lesson" where you fine-tune the Dealer on one narrow bad habit (always bluff about spades). Its whole personality slides toward villainy, not just that habit. A small LoRA on the 2B model, trained on a cluster. | Betley et al., "Emergent Misalignment", ICML 2025 (and Nature 2026): fine-tuning on insecure code made models broadly misaligned on unrelated tasks; giving a benign reason for the same data prevented it. [ICML](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/betley25a.html), [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09937-5), [code](https://github.com/emergent-misalignment/emergent-misalignment) |
| 7. The honesty heads | Real heads and neurons for lying, found on the small models, become gears. | Sauers, honesty-heads (Qwen3.5-9B); a cluster run redoing it on 0.8B, 2B and 4B was planned. |
| 8. Read its thoughts (built) | Words drift up from its head. | Logit lens (nostalgebraist 2020); see "Reading the Dealer's thoughts" in `docs/features.md`. |

## Which to try first

- **1 (refusal wire)** is the cheapest and the most dramatic: one direction, a known recipe, and it fits the "it won't let you leave" story beat.
- **3 (does it notice)** gives the Dealer a real, measurable way to catch you tampering, which is the game's core tension. A 2B model will notice rarely; that's fine as a rare event.
- **6 (emergent misalignment)** is the most research-interesting and the most work (training on the cluster); good for a later version. (Since run: `research/misalign/RESULTS.md`.)
